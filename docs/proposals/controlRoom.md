# The twin's control room

**Status: agreed, with the four open decisions taken as recommended. All six steps are built, and the tuning card's live twin is retired: the card is about tuning again, its replay goal offering **Open the live twin** with its log (another logger's table handed over as the card read it, in Konjugate's own form). Step 6, matching by meaning, locally, was built only after measuring rules and memory on names written as plants and vendors write them ([tests/fixtures/plantSignalNames.mjs](../../tests/fixtures/plantSignalNames.mjs), sixty signals on ten example models): they found the right quantity for 43 of 57 but were sure enough to fill in only 3, and filled in one wrong (line 3's flow as line 1's). They fell short for three reasons, none of which needed a language model: evidence a source does not give (no readings, no behaviour seen) counted against a match, so a name alone never reached 75%; numbers were dropped from names, so line 1 and line 3 looked alike; and the vocabulary lacked the words engineers use (SOC, OAT, LVDT, odometry, omega, TC, case for housing, inflow). With only the evidence there is weighed, numbers that must agree, a vocabulary of concepts and abbreviations read by their first letters or their consonants (trq for torque, rm for room), all 57 are right, 43 filled in, none wrong, and the signals the models have nothing for left alone. Names written afterwards and not used to tune it (twenty-two on five models) are right for 17 of 20, 11 filled in, none wrong; the three it misses need knowing that a payload is the suspended mass and a shaker table the fixed support, which a language model might know, so a local one stays the next step if such names turn out to be common ([tests/signalMatchingCorpus.test.mjs](../../tests/signalMatchingCorpus.test.mjs) keeps both measured). Step 5 keeps the tiles' order, width, pins and hidden ones, the window's place (on a second screen, kept by the project window when the twin's window closes, and clamped onto a screen that is there when it opens), and the last run's events, with the project; the time slider scrubs back through the last thousand moves. Step 4, the engineer's view, is built: tunable parameters as sliders applied from the latest move and kept in the model, refit, the warning taken at once, what a move does with each signal, the estimator's noise, drift, ensemble and tracked parameters, and the timing (taken when the twin starts again); a model changed in the project window is offered with what changed and applied from the latest move (src/windowSession.mjs, applyModel), each state still in the model carried across by its id and a new one at its initial value, and a change removing a mapped state or input parameter refused (decision 4). Building it showed that a value the engineer keeps in the model must not come back from the project window as a change to offer, so the room keeps the model it last took and expects that change; and that a recording the twin made must come back with the mapping it was made with, since it carries the twin's names and the raw values. Steps 1, 2 and 3 are built. Step 3, the operator view, is built: the health along the top, a tile for every measured signal, and under them the inputs as step lines, what the twin infers (every state no sensor measures, reported by the twin's engine after the measured ones and sent to the room thinned to 80 points a move, with its trend over the last hour of data; with the estimator, its spread and the parameters it tracks), and the events, one timed list. The project window's toolbar chip follows the twin window's twin and brings its window forward; the tuning card's own twin mode, its introduction and its demonstrations are gone, and its live twin is reached from the replay goal until step 4 moves what is left of it (the estimator, refit, the sandbox and scenarios) into the room. An input is sent as its changes, so a step is kept however often it is read. Building it showed that without the estimator, the rig's unmeasured states (the heater's heat flow, the room's temperature) are only what the model carries, so the room says so beside each; a heat loss found growing (the proposal's "heat loss 27 W/K, rising") needs the estimator tracking it, which the engineer's view turns on in step 4. The time scrubbed back, and the events kept with the twin's record, are step 5's, with the layout. Step 2, the setup in the twin's own window, is built (`src/twinWindow/`, the matcher `src/signalMatching.mjs`, the setup kept as `twin` by `src/twinSetup.mjs`, conversions applied as readings reach the twin): the Live twin button opens it, its three numbered steps are on one page, the sources discover what they offer, and the mapping is filled in from suggestions at 75% or more, the others waiting for a click. Building it showed that a role the source declares (a Konjugate data log says which signals are inputs) is the strongest evidence after the name, both to rule a candidate out and to support it; that an input's plausibility is judged by its readings nearest the model's value, since it is often off when looked at; that an instrument tag's loop number has two digits or more, or a tank numbered 1 (TK1) is read as a temperature tag; and that the rig's readings offered against another model match nothing, so the window offers to open the rig's model in the project window. [tests/engine/twinSetup.mjs](../../tests/engine/twinSetup.mjs) matches the plant-named recording to the rig's model by the suggestions alone, with their conversions, and the twin makes the same moves as from the rig's own recording. Step 1, the unit catalogue, is built (`src/units.mjs`, tested in `tests/units.test.mjs`): it reads every unit the example models use, and building it settled three things. An angle is a dimension of its own, so rad/s and Hz are not taken for one another; between an angular speed and a frequency one revolution is taken as one cycle (60 rpm is 1 Hz and 2π rad/s), and an angle onto a model's plain number is taken as radians, each conversion saying so. As engineers write them, everything after a slash is below it (W/m2K is W/(m²·K)), and symbols written together are split (kgK, Nm). And units that look alike are kept apart where they differ: a torr is 1/760 of an atmosphere, a millimetre of mercury a little more; a bare C on its own is read as degrees Celsius, as instrument tags mean it, and says so.** Everything a live twin does is built ([Digital twin connections](digitalTwinConnections.md), [the comparison window](predictionActualWindow.md), [the estimator](stateEstimator.md), [the push service](pushService.md)); what is not right yet is how it is presented. Today a twin is set up and watched in one side card that was made for parameter tuning: cramped, setup and watching mixed together, and only the first measured signal is plotted, so a twin with five sensors shows one. This document designs what a person sitting in a control room expects instead: a short setup once, and then a full window of graphs for every quantity they want to evaluate.

## Who sits in front of it

Two people, often the same one at different moments.

- **An operator** watches: is the real system doing what the model says, is anything drifting, is the data coming in. They need to read the room at a glance and must not be able to break the twin with a stray click. The operator view is the default.
- **An engineer** takes over when something is off: refits, tracks a parameter, sets a tunable parameter to a new value, or goes back to the model and changes it. The engineer's controls are one switch away, not a login.

## One twin, one window, three steps to it

A twin has its own window, one twin per window. A twin compares a model with a running system, where several controllable quantities come in as inputs to the simulation and several sensors' outputs are compared with the model's states; all of them belong to the one twin. The model is always the open project: the twin's window belongs to a project window, and the model can be read, and changed, there beside it.

The Live twin button in the project window opens the twin's window: at its setup the first time, at its control room once it is set up. The setup is three steps, saved with the project, and opened again only to change something.

1. **Model.** The open project, with what it offers a twin: its states (with units and descriptions) to compare with sensors, and its shared parameters to drive with inputs. A model with nothing to compare against says so here, with what to add.
2. **Source.** The four ways in (a data log, an MQTT broker, an OPC UA server, readings a script sends), and Konjugate connects and discovers what comes in: the file's columns, the topics a broker carries in a few seconds, the server's tags with their units and ranges, the signals the push service's keys may send. Each incoming signal is listed with its latest value and its unit, when the source gives one.
3. **Mapping.** Each incoming signal against the model, in one table:
   - its **role**: an input (driving a shared parameter), measured (compared with a state), or not used;
   - **the model quantity** it maps to, with **a suggestion**, its confidence and its reason ("*TT101.PV* → *Water tank · Temperature*, 92%: T and PV in the tag, °C is a temperature, its range fits the tank's");
   - **the unit conversion**, proposed for the person to confirm (below);
   - **a preview** for each mapped pair: a few minutes of the signal against the model's run on the inputs mapped so far, as a sparkline, so a wrong sensor or a wrong unit is seen before the twin starts;
   - for a large source (a server with two hundred tags), **accept every confident suggestion** at once, and a search box for the rest.

Then **Open the control room**.

## Finding the matches: rules and memory

The names a source uses rarely match a model's (`TT101.PV`, `Tank1_Temp`, `ns=2;s=Line3/Heater/Pwr` against "Water tank · Temperature"). Suggestions come from evidence that can be explained, cheapest first, and the person confirms.

1. **The name, normalised:** case, separators, camelCase, and the common decorations (`.PV`, `_meas`, `Value`, a path's last part).
2. **Abbreviations and synonyms:** temp, T and TT for temperature, pwr and P for power, lvl and LT for level, FT and Q for flow, PT for pressure, and the ISA letter codes of instrument tags (the first letter the quantity, TT a temperature transmitter, TC a controller). A small dictionary, built in, and extended by the person or a domain package.
3. **The unit's dimension:** a temperature can map to a temperature in any of its units, never to a pressure; a source's unit, and an OPC UA tag's engineering unit and range, are evidence on their own.
4. **Plausible values:** the signal's range against what the state takes (its initial value, its tuning bounds, a quick run of the model).
5. **Behaviour:** after a minute of data, a signal that steps (what an operator does) is likely an input, a smooth one a measurement; and optionally a candidate pair's correlation over the preview, the strongest evidence of all.
6. **Memory:** every match confirmed is remembered for that source, by its own name for the signal (`TT101.PV` → the tank's temperature), and offered first the next time, and to the next rig whose tags follow the same scheme. Kept in the app's own folder, as well as in the project.

Semantic matching (similarity of the names, paths and descriptions as meanings, not strings) is left for later and would run locally only: tag names can be confidential plant information, and a lab may be offline. Rules and memory may well be enough; it is built only if they are not.

## Units, converted and proposed

Every mapping carries a conversion, proposed when the units differ and confirmed by the person: °C and °F to K, kPa, bar and psi to Pa, L/min and gpm to m³/s, kW and hp to W, % to a fraction, rpm to rad/s, mA to A, and the compound units models use (W/K, J/(kg·K), m³/h). A conversion is a scale and an offset, as reading another logger's file already uses (`src/loggerCsv.mjs`), so nothing downstream changes. A raw transmitter signal (a 4–20 mA loop, counts from an ADC) is scaled through the range the source gives for it (an OPC UA tag's instrument and engineering ranges) or the person enters. Two dimensions that cannot match (a pressure against a temperature) are not proposed, and a mapping made by hand across them is flagged.

This needs a catalogue of units that parses what people write (`degC`, `°C`, `deg C`, `W/(m·K)`, `m3/h`) into dimensions and factors. Whether to write a small one or take an established library is open decision 1. Model units are free text today ([project schema](../projectSchema.md)), so the catalogue also lets the model editor say when a unit is not one it knows.

## The control room

The twin's window, maximized in the workspace, or moved to a second screen.

- **Health, along the top:** the twin (live, how far behind the data, how many times faster than real time it runs), the source (connected, readings a second, the age of the latest reading, reconnections), the drift warning, and the model's name and when it last changed.
- **A strip for every measured signal, in one column, on one time axis**: the measured readings against the model, the window shaded, the forecast with its band, and the model's past as each move ran it (broken between moves, so a correction is seen as a jump); in the strip's header its latest value, the jumps as a sparkline and the tolerance, and down its left edge a bar that reads across the room: green in step, amber leaning, red past the warning. Under the strips, at the foot of the column, a short step strip for each input, in its own units; under them the one time axis. Every strip has the same margins, so a time is in the same place in each: a step in an input sits under the bend it makes in a reading. Pointing at a time marks it through the whole column, each strip and input saying its value there and the time beneath. A strip's options are one button, always there, opening a menu: pin it to the top, draw it taller (each with a check when on), or hide it; strips are dragged to reorder. A strip is never under 140 pixels, so a column of many scrolls. The plots show the span chosen in the room's header (Show): by default four times the twin's window, so what is being watched stays at a readable scale however long the twin has run; or the last 15 minutes, hour, 6 hours, day or week; or everything since the first reading. The span runs on to the forecast's end, only what is in it is drawn, and the axis is in seconds, minutes, hours or days to suit it; it is kept with the layout. What the twin infers and the events are in a sidebar beside the column; the Engineer button puts the engineer's view in the events' place, what the twin infers staying above it. Building it showed that a tile per signal, each with its own axis and the inputs in a panel of their own, let no one line an input's step up with the bend it caused, and that a model drawn only over its latest window hid the claim the twin rests on: that it stayed in step for hours.
- **What the twin infers:** the states no sensor measures and the parameters it tracks, each with its value, spread and trend ("heat loss 27 W/K, rising").
- **Events:** warnings, reconnections, readings out of time order, a source's sampling limit, refits applied, changes to the model; one list, timed, kept with the twin's record.
- **Time:** following now, and scrubbed back through the record to see what happened.

The layout is saved with the project, so the next shift opens the same room.

## The engineer's view, and changing the model

The **Engineer** switch in the control room shows what the operator view keeps out of the way: refit on the latest window, the estimator's settings, which parameters it tracks, and the tunable parameters themselves, as sliders within their bounds.

Every change, small or large, is a change to the model, applied to the twin from its latest move, so there is one model and one way changes reach the twin:

- **From the control room:** moving a tunable parameter's slider, or accepting a refit, changes that value in the model, and the twin starts again from its latest move with it (as applying a refit already does).
- **From the project window:** an engineer changes anything there (an equation, a relationship, a new state), and the control room says the model has changed, with **Apply to the twin**. Applied, the twin starts again from its latest move, carrying the states that still exist across, with the new ones at their initial values, and checks the mapping still holds: a mapped state that was removed is said, not dropped in silence. When the states changed too much to carry across, the recent window is run again from the recording instead.
- **Every change is an event** in the twin's record, saying what changed, from what to what, and when, so a twin's history can be followed and repeated.

## What moves where

The setup and the control room replace the live twin's part of the tuning card. Replaying a log once and calibrating on it stay in the tuning card, which goes back to being about tuning; the twin's setup offers both for its own source. The sandbox (detach and try), scenarios, the estimator, refit and the push service's Connections panel are reached from the control room, the first two in the operator view (trying something does not change the twin), the rest in the engineer's.

## Demonstrations

- **A motor, in real time** (built: [scripts/generateMotorTwin.mjs](../../scripts/generateMotorTwin.mjs), [tests/engine/motorTwin.mjs](../../tests/engine/motorTwin.mjs)): the slow demonstrations show a twin keeping in step over hours, but at a second a second nothing moves for minutes, and a joystick on a tank's heater changes nothing anyone can see. A DC motor answers its voltage within half a second: three minutes of an operator at its controller, played in real time, its twin set up with the project (a five-second window moving every second, five seconds of forecast, a millisecond's step), its current inferred, and its bearing warming from a minute and a half in, so the twin warns of the drift. Its voltage is a live shared parameter: the input to bind a joystick or keys to in Try something, where Follow the controls at real time shows the dotted line bend within a second. The Demonstrations dialog chooses how fast each recording arrives (each its own pace by default: the motor in real time, the tank at 600×, the house at 1200×), and the room reads its times in the unit that suits them (seconds for the motor, hours for the tank).
- The simulated rig as now, one click from the Live twin button, opening straight into its control room.
- **A rig named as a plant would name it:** the simulated rig's recording with plant-style tag names and units (`TT101.PV` in °C, `JI101.PV` for the heater's power in kW), so the mapping step's suggestions and proposed conversions can be seen working on a source whose names match nothing in the model.
- Two screens: the project window on one, the control room on the other, an equation changed in the model and applied to the running twin.
- **A house** (built: [scripts/generateHouseTwin.mjs](../../scripts/generateHouseTwin.mjs), [tests/engine/houseTwin.mjs](../../tests/engine/houseTwin.mjs)): three rooms with their heaters and thermostats, the weather and the sun, a day of the controller's recording in °C, for a room with many tiles and inputs. The bedroom window is left open from 14:00 to 18:00, and the bedroom drifts past the warning while the living room and the kitchen stay in step. Building it showed that the drift was judged on the first measured signal only, so a twin of several sensors now judges each, the move's own trend being the one that matters most; that a warning must mean a lean, not large jumps scattered either side of nothing; that a tolerance must stand above the sensor's noise (now at least four times it); and that the matcher must rank by its whole score before capping the confidence it shows, or the living room's thermometer and the outdoor air (both temperatures, both of the right size) tie.

## Order of work

1. **The unit catalogue**, with conversions proposed and dimensions checked, and its tests.
2. **The setup in the twin's own window:** model, source with discovery, and the mapping table with rule-based suggestions, memory, conversions and previews; the plant-named rig demonstration.
3. **The control room, operator view:** health, a tile for every measured signal, inputs, inferred quantities, events.
4. **The engineer's view and changes to the model** applied from the latest move, from the control room and from the project window, recorded as events.
5. **The layout** arranged, pinned, saved, on a second screen, and time scrubbed back.
6. **Semantic matching, locally**, only if rules and memory fall short.

## Decisions

Taken as recommended when the design was agreed.

1. **The unit catalogue:** a small one written for Konjugate (a few hundred units and the compound forms models use, fully under our control and easy to check), or an established library. Proposed: written here, with its tests, since the units of engineering signals are a bounded set and a library would bring more than is needed.
2. **What the operator view may do:** proposed, watch, scrub through time and detach to try something (which never changes the twin); everything that changes the twin is the engineer's.
3. **Where remembered matches live:** proposed, the app's own folder, shared across projects, as well as the project's own mapping.
4. **A model change that cannot be carried across** (states removed that were mapped): proposed, say so and refuse to apply until the mapping is fixed in the setup, rather than apply part of it.
